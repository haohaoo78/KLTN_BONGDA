# Tournament group draw workflow

The tournament draw is owned by `tournament-service`; authorized tournament managers preview, save, confirm, and publish drafts through the existing `/tournaments/:id/draw-management/**` API. Draw candidates come from the Organization service's approved and roster-locked registrations. The server revalidates eligibility, capacity, group balance, uniqueness, seed pots, and institution constraints before accepting each change.

## Methods

The format and round settings are part of the published tournament rules. Round-robin formats store one or two legs at creation; fixture generation uses that saved setting. Group-and-knockout formats store the group capacity, advancing teams per group, and either an automatic group count or a fixed group count. The draw service derives/validates the group count from the qualified teams and published capacity; round-robin is constrained to one group. Group allocation can be balanced or fill earlier groups first, with random or seeded draw preference and a same-institution policy (none, prefer separation when feasible, or require separation). These rules are added through the version 15 Tournament migration; old tournaments receive the JSON defaults and their existing structured fields remain intact.

Tournament creation also stores the knockout tie-break policy (`PENALTIES_ONLY` or `EXTRA_TIME_THEN_PENALTIES`) and total extra-time minutes, along with the expected number of playable pitches, operating hours per day, and allowed kickoff window. The Match scheduling service rejects kickoffs outside that window in Vietnam time. The creation form uses the saved rules, regulation match duration/rest interval, maximum team count, and format to show an indicative round/match/day-capacity preflight. This is a planning estimate only: actual venue availability and exact team rest constraints are validated again in schedule management, where fixtures and kickoff times are known.

The additional knockout settings (one/two legs, third-place match, BYE handling, and hybrid pairing preference) are persisted with the tournament configuration and restored when the draft is edited. Bracket fixture generation currently supports single-elimination with one match per tie, structural BYEs, and—when configured—one third-place fixture linked to the losing sources of both semifinals. Two-leg aggregate knockout ties are not generated by the Match service yet.

- **Manual:** assign each qualified team to one group. The draft must contain every qualified team exactly once and match the configured group-allocation sizes.
- **Random:** the Tournament service creates a balanced randomized allocation. For a live ceremony, the server creates and keeps the full allocation private, then reveals one already-validated assignment per transaction; the browser never receives the unrevealed order.
- **Seed pots:** the organizer explicitly assigns each qualified team to a pot. The service draws teams from those pots, places at most one team from a pot in each group, and rejects a pot larger than the number of groups. Seed numbers are independent of the team's display order.

The optional institution rule is a hard constraint: if the service cannot find an allocation satisfying it, it returns a conflict and does not quietly publish a violating draw. Manual groups and generated previews can be adjusted before saving, then are validated again by the server.

Institution separation applies to group allocation, not the single knockout bracket. A legacy `sameInstitutionPolicy` value in published knockout regulations does not enable group separation during draw preview, draft validation, or live allocation. The regulations remain unchanged; one-bracket capacity, team uniqueness and fixed seed positions are still validated.

## Lifecycle and safety

### Live ceremony presentation

Step 3 uses the same persisted live picks for both the latest revealed ticket and the allocation diagram. Each successful pick fills its assigned diagram position immediately; the latest position has a visible “Vừa bốc” label and border. The diagram appears directly beneath the ceremony controls, before the remaining-team pool. One collapsed history below the diagram retains the pick sequence and a nested ceremony audit log. Audit events are not additional allocations; the published draw remains the official result. This presentation change does not alter allocation, persistence, publication, or Step 4 behavior.

Once the live result is published, ceremony setup, tickets, remaining-team pool and editing actions are hidden. A completion notice provides a button to Step 4, with the collapsed ceremony history immediately below it and the read-only allocation diagram following. After the final pick returns a published result, the official BTC workspace saves Step 4 through the existing workflow API, then reloads the page to open the fixture diagram with fresh backend data. Failed publication does not redirect; failed step navigation preserves the result and displays an error with the existing navigation button available. Opening an already completed ceremony for review does not trigger another reload.

Drafts are persisted as versioned records. Confirmation and publication are separate actions for the ordinary manual/random/seed-pot workflow; publishing uses the confirmed version. Published and confirmed versions are not overwritten by editing a new draft. A subsequent version requires a recorded change reason, and history remains available. Draw mutation is limited to the existing tournament-manager authorization and the tournament's roster-locked stage.

### Method locks and independent drafts

Random/seeded and manual drafts are stored independently by method; switching methods resumes that method's own draft. Tournament-service serializes draft, schedule, confirm, and publish mutations with the tournament advisory lock, and rejects writes when a live schedule/session is active or a published result already exists. There can be only one published draw per tournament. Publishing a random/manual result archives all unused draft/confirmed versions in the same transaction.

Publishing a direct-draw schedule locks random/manual changes immediately, while still allowing BTC to update or postpone that schedule. Before the first pick, BTC may cancel only after supplying a reason; cancellation is audited and unlocks the other methods while preserving compatible drafts. Once a pick is recorded, cancellation is rejected. The last direct pick and official publication are committed atomically; that transaction archives remaining drafts and makes the result immutable through the normal workflow. Step 4 reads only the published version.

Tournament migration 23 adds the direct-session location and cancellation-reason fields and indexes enforcing at most one active draft per method and one published result per tournament. It is additive and does not reset or rewrite existing tournament data.

For a pure knockout tournament, the one allocation group represents bracket entry order; actual pairings and BYEs are generated and displayed in the separate Fixture Diagram stage. For group-and-knockout tournaments, generating the diagram creates group fixtures and reserves the playoff bracket topology with stable Match IDs. The slots show qualification sources (for example, first place in Group A) until official standings are confirmed.

Fixture generation and public release are separate states. Generated group or knockout fixtures are stored as a `DRAFT` generation and are visible to authorized BTC managers through `/tournaments/:tournamentId/fixtures/manage`, but are excluded from public match lists, bracket reads, match detail, live-feed, lineup, event, and report reads. BTC publishes the complete required structure using `POST /tournaments/:tournamentId/fixtures/publish` with an empty JSON body. The Match service derives required stages from the tournament's pinned regulation and validates the expected fixture count, unique round-robin pairs, no self-play, and stage/draw/regulation version before publishing all required stages in one transaction. This makes hybrid GROUP + KNOCKOUT publication atomic. The endpoint is idempotent and keeps the original publisher/time. Legacy generations are migrated as `PUBLISHED` to preserve existing public data. Schedule creation and updates reject a fixture stage that is still a draft.

Knockout fixture generation creates one real match record for every playable bracket slot, including later rounds whose entrants are not known yet. Those rows use nullable participants (no fabricated team IDs) and are linked to bracket slots. BTC can reserve a date, venue, and referee crew for them before the preceding match finishes. A match remains unavailable for lineups, kickoff, and result entry until its slot has two resolved entrants. When an approved result advances a winner, the existing match row is filled in place so its schedule and referee assignments remain attached. BYE slots do not create playable matches.

For a group-and-knockout tournament, playoff entrants are derived only after every group result is official and the configured ranking criteria have resolved all ties. The bracket topology and schedulable Match IDs exist earlier, but participant slots remain unresolved until that point. Once qualifiers are confirmed, the service fills the existing slots and Match rows rather than creating a second bracket. Structural BYEs propagate only after qualification is known; no team is invented or advanced from an unresolved result. Scheduling or referee assignment remains subject to the normal bracket-readiness and tournament publication rules.

Ordinary match results are made official by the referee appointed with final authority. A referee can explicitly route an exceptional report to the organizer; only those reports remain pending for organizer review. Result corrections continue through the audited organizer decision flow.

## Operator workflow

1. Select a tournament whose roster and regulation are finalized.
2. Pick Manual, Random, or Seed Pots. For Seed Pots, explicitly assign every team; the display sequence is not treated as a seed.
3. Review the group count and school-separation rule inherited from the tournament regulations; the server rejects a configuration that cannot fit the locked team count.
4. For ordinary publication, preview/adjust, save a draft, confirm, and publish. For a direct ceremony, enter event name, date/time, timezone and announcement, then publish the schedule. The service stores a hidden server-generated allocation and validates the registration snapshot before the session can start.
5. At the scheduled instant, BTC explicitly starts the ceremony. Every “next pick” request uses the expected sequence and an idempotency key; the database transaction records the pick, history and state before sending a PostgreSQL notification. Repeated/stale requests cannot create a second pick. Pause/resume and schedule changes are audited; a started session cannot be cancelled or redrawn.
6. The final pick automatically publishes the official draw version in the same transaction and unlocks the Fixture Diagram stage. No second confirm/publish action is required for a direct ceremony. A database or roster conflict leaves the session intact for review and does not silently replace picks.
7. Public tournament pages fetch the safe persisted snapshot on entry/reconnect and subscribe to SSE invalidations. The stream contains no hidden assignments; a 15-second snapshot refresh recovers dropped events. Schedule and pick state use server time, while the browser countdown is only an indication.
8. Open the Fixture Diagram stage to generate pairings from the published allocation. Schedule dates, times, venues and referees only against those fixture IDs.

Live draw manager endpoints are under `/tournaments/:tournamentId/draw-management/live-session`; scheduling mutations are `POST .../live-session`, `PATCH .../live-session/schedule`, starting is `POST .../live-session/start`, picks are `POST .../live-session/next`, pause/resume use `PATCH .../live-session`. The public read-only snapshot is `/tournaments/:tournamentId/live-draw`, and the public event stream is `/tournaments/:tournamentId/live-draw/stream`. Tournament migration 22 adds schedule fields, audited event history and idempotency keys while preserving existing session rows. The last pick and official `PUBLISHED` version are committed atomically. The legacy finalize endpoint is read/repair compatibility only; it does not re-publish a draft.

The frontend supports drag/drop on desktop and team-then-group selection on touch devices. The backend remains authoritative for every invariant and permission check.

## Step 4 — Fixture diagram

Step 4 is available only when Step 3 has a `PUBLISHED` draw version. Publishing Step 3 pins a snapshot of the competition format, ranking criteria, rules, and regulation version into the draw version's existing JSON config. The workspace reads that exact version and its group assignments as read-only input; it does not offer team selection, a second draw, or group reassignment. Fixture generation reuses the Match service's idempotent generation API and stable match IDs. Match records persist stage, group, round number, leg, draw version, and deterministic fixture key; fixture generations also retain the matching regulation version. The organizer can inspect the generated structure, run structural checks, then confirm publication; Step 5 unlocks only after every required stage for the latest published draw is published (both GROUP and KNOCKOUT for a hybrid tournament).

Round-robin and hybrid views display the official group allocation, Berger round cards, leg/group/team filters, resting teams for odd-sized groups, readonly results, and standings. Hybrid view adds unresolved rank-based qualification slots before the bracket; allocation positions such as A1 are not treated as final standings. Knockout view displays bracket sources and BYEs separately from playable matches. Demo-only group score inputs are stored in browser localStorage and are never sent to Match APIs; official results are read from approved Match results. When the rules use FAIR_PLAY, the BTC-only `/tournaments/:tournamentId/fixtures/group-fair-play` read endpoint returns Match-service card penalties for the currently published draw. The UI displays these values in the standings and uses fewer penalty points as the better fair-play result, matching playoff ranking.

The Match migration version 20 adds bracket slot type and source-outcome fields without changing existing rows. Version 21 adds normalized stage/round/leg metadata and a regulation-version column, backfilling metadata where the existing draw and bracket records identify it. For a configured third-place match, fixture generation creates a stable match ID with two explicit loser-source links to the semifinal slots. Once each semifinal result is approved, the real losing team is resolved into that existing match row; no losing entrant is guessed during Step 4. Two-leg knockout ties remain unsupported and must not be presented as operational until that workflow is implemented.

The UI build can be checked without a published Step 3 draw, but an end-to-end Step 4 review requires an official published draw. The draw-demo tournament's localStorage preview is intentionally not accepted as official Step 3 output and therefore leaves Step 4 locked until BTC publishes a real draw version. LocalStorage workspaces remain confined to the explicitly labeled DEV demo panel; normal tournament draw and live ceremony APIs read only the locked official roster and store changes in Tournament service.

In development/test, the BTC workspace may additionally expose the isolated `DrawDemo*` flow when the test-configuration API explicitly enables it. It uses eligible teams/rules from server APIs and server-side test snapshots, and never changes the official draw/fixture tables. Test API read failures other than the expected 404 are surfaced; the official production path continues to use the Tournament and Match APIs described above.

## Actor notifications and live updates

The Notification service emits an authenticated, user-scoped SSE invalidation event when a notification row is inserted. The event contains only the notification ID; clients reload notification content and unread counts through the existing authenticated APIs. This updates notification bells and the notification inbox, and signals mounted tournament registration, team tournament, schedule, match operations, and tournament detail panels to refetch their authorized data. A 30-second recovery refresh is used for notification surfaces if the stream is unavailable. Existing domain workflows remain responsible for creating notifications for the actors affected by a decision or schedule/result change; the stream does not expose another user's notifications or act as a replacement for domain authorization. Match live-feed SSE and public live-draw SSE remain separate streams for unauthenticated spectators.

The Match service's `/matches/live/stream` is also used as a public invalidation feed. Schedule creation, change, cancellation, deletion, referee crew replacement, live match events, and official result changes write a realtime event in the same database transaction as the domain change. Public tournament details and mounted organizer/referee match and schedule panels subscribe and refetch through their normal APIs; the feed carries no roster or private registration payload. The API Gateway permits this read-only stream without a session, while schedule and match mutations remain authenticated and ownership-checked by Match service.

## Interactive draw Sandbox (UI review)

The BTC registration-review page offers **Mở Sandbox chia bảng** so the draw UI can be reviewed even while the real draw step is locked. This is a browser-only prototype in `DrawSandboxPanel`: its 32-team pool and assignments are mock data; generated match counts, BYEs, groups, knockout placeholders, viewer roles, and live-session states are simulations. Saved Sandbox drafts use this browser's `localStorage`; reset removes only the Sandbox key. It does not call tournament draw, live-session, fixture, or publication APIs, and it never changes tournament registrations or official draw data.

The Sandbox exposes Round Robin, Knockout, and Mixed formats with Live, Random, and Manual allocation, including team selection, group count/allocation, round-robin legs, qualifiers, third-place option, seed pots, school separation, a manual group editor, bracket/group preview, and simulated waiting/live/paused/disconnected/completed states. This preview supports design feedback for the nine format/method combinations; it is not a sandbox runtime service and is not an operational alternative to the authorized production draw workflow above. The production path continues to source only locked, approved teams from services.

The in-page allocation demo has separate browser `localStorage` drafts for Direct, Random, and Manual, plus a local control record for scheduled/started/official lock states. Its publish and cancellation actions are simulations only: they do not publish the live schedule, notify actors, update the tournament lifecycle, or unlock Step 4. The official BTC workflow uses Tournament-service persistence and server-side access checks described above.
